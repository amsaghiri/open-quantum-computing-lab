Learn

12 textbook chapters develop the mathematics, physical intuition, circuit model, algorithms, noise, NISQ methods, communication, and reproducibility.

Experiment

24 projects progress from 15-minute micro-experiments to a 5–10 hour capstone.

Publish reproducibly

Students apply for projects by email and submit notebooks, raw data, figures, reports, and environment details through Pull Requests.

How students work

Every project follows the same scientific routine, beginning on a simulator and using real hardware only when it is useful and approved.

  1. Learn
  2. Predict
  3. Reproduce the baseline
  4. Measure
  5. Extend with one controlled change
  6. Analyze
  7. Submit reproducibly

About this educational site. This website is designed for teaching and learning quantum computing. It does not aim to reproduce materials from other educational platforms. Students may use any suitable quantum simulator or computing environment. We thank qBraid QUEST for supporting quantum computing education at William Paterson University this semester. Some supplementary references and activities have been aligned with QUEST resources to support course assignments, while OQCL remains platform-neutral and students are free to choose other suitable tools.

Teacher notes — Session 2: Measurement, gates, and interference

For instructorsFlipped format

The session in one sentence. Gates change amplitudes reversibly, measurement samples them, and amplitudes with opposite signs cancel.

The student page for Session 2 is written for self-study: students can read, work the example, run both experiments, and check their own answers without you. These notes are for the class time you do have.

Before class (students, on their own)

  • Read Chapter 2.
  • Do the worked example and the first experiment on the student page.
  • Bring one written prediction and one question.

If you have 20 minutes

MinutesWhat to do
0–3Ask the exit question below cold, and take a show of hands. Do not give the answer yet.
3–10Board: the worked example from the student page, with students supplying each step.
10–16Live demo: run the second experiment. Collect predictions first, then run it.
16–20Return to the exit question, settle it, and assign the project.

MinutesActivity
0–10Motivating question and show of hands.
10–30The three points below, with the mathematics on the board.
30–45Worked example at the board, students supplying steps.

Three points to make

  1. Measurement is sampling: show 10 shots versus 1,000 shots.
  2. HH = I on the board, tracking the signs.
  3. Insert Z: no probability changes, yet the answer flips. That is interference.

Board example

Follow the amplitudes through H, Z, H starting from |0⟩.

  1. H|0⟩ = (|0⟩ + |1⟩)/√2.
  2. Z flips the sign of |1⟩: (|0⟩ − |1⟩)/√2. The probabilities are still 50/50.
  3. Apply H to each part: H|0⟩ = (|0⟩ + |1⟩)/√2 and H|1⟩ = (|0⟩ − |1⟩)/√2.
  4. Combine: ½[(|0⟩ + |1⟩) − (|0⟩ − |1⟩)] = ½[2|1⟩] = |1⟩.

The two |0⟩ contributions cancel and the two |1⟩ contributions add. Without the Z, the signs are the other way round and the result is |0⟩.

Misconceptions to raise

  • Reading H as “makes the qubit random”. A random bit stays random after a second H; a superposition does not.
  • Thinking more shots change the state. Shots only improve your estimate of the same probabilities.
  • Assuming Z “does nothing” because it leaves the probabilities of |0⟩ and |1⟩ unchanged.

Discussion prompts

  • How is a superposition different from “we just do not know the value yet”?
  • Which experiment could convince a sceptic that the qubit after one H is not simply a random bit?

Exit question

Ask: Why does H followed by H always give 0, when one H gives a random result?

Looking for: The two paths to |1⟩ carry opposite signs and cancel, while the two paths to |0⟩ add.

One project from this session, following the predict → baseline → one extension → analyze routine. Project solutions are deliberately not published on this site.

Teacher notes — Session 7: Communication, benchmarking, and your own project

For instructorsFlipped format

The session in one sentence. BB84 detects eavesdropping because measuring in the wrong basis disturbs the state, and honest results need recorded evidence.

The student page for Session 7 is written for self-study: students can read, work the example, run both experiments, and check their own answers without you. These notes are for the class time you do have.

Before class (students, on their own)

  • Read Chapter 11 and Chapter 12.
  • Do the worked example and the first experiment on the student page.
  • Bring one written prediction and one question.

If you have 20 minutes

Three points to make

  1. Two bases, random choices, keep only matching rounds.
  2. Why Eve causes 25% errors: two halves multiplied, on the board.
  3. What a benchmark number does and does not tell you.

Board example

How much error does an eavesdropper cause? Look only at rounds where Alice and Bob used the same basis.

  1. Eve guesses the basis. She is right half the time, and then causes no error.
  2. The other half, she measures in the wrong basis and sends on a state in that wrong basis. Bob, measuring in Alice’s basis, now gets a random result and is wrong half the time.
  3. Error rate in the sifted key: ½ × ½ = ¼, or 25%.
  4. If Alice and Bob compare k bits, Eve escapes detection on each with probability ¾. For k = 20 that is (¾)²⁰ ≈ 0.003, about 0.3%.

Misconceptions to raise

  • Thinking the basis announcement leaks the key. Bases are revealed only after the measurements, and the bit values never are.
  • Keeping the bits used for the error check. They were announced publicly and must be discarded.
  • Treating a simulated protocol as a security proof, or a single benchmark run as a general statement about a device.

Discussion prompts

  • What does BB84 assume about the devices that a real attacker might violate?
  • What would you need to see before trusting a published hardware benchmark?

Exit question

Ask: In BB84 with an intercept-and-resend eavesdropper, what error rate appears in the sifted key?

Looking for: About 25%: she picks the wrong basis half the time, and then Bob is wrong half the time.

One project from this session, following the predict → baseline → one extension → analyze routine. Project solutions are deliberately not published on this site.

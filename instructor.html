Instructor Guide

OQCL is designed to support teaching rather than replace the instructor. A suggested class rhythm is motivate → explain → derive → predict → demonstrate → discuss limitations → assign experiment → analyze evidence.

Suggested 75-minute class

TimeActivity
0–10Use case and motivating question
10–30Concept and mathematics
30–45Worked example at the board
45–55Misconception/problem discussion
55–65Mini-demo or prediction
65–75Project briefing and exit question

Teaching principles

  • Do not lead with a vendor UI. Lead with a scientific question.
  • Require a prediction before code.
  • Use simulators for understanding and debugging; use QPUs when hardware behavior is itself part of the lesson.
  • Separate mathematical approximation error, finite-shot uncertainty, compilation effects, and physical noise.
  • Require classical baselines for optimization/ML claims when feasible.
  • Never equate a small demonstration with quantum advantage.
  • Preserve raw data and metadata.

External laboratories

OQCL may send students to an official external tutorial or hardware environment for platform-specific execution. Students then return to OQCL for interpretation, extension, and submission. External instructions remain external and are cited rather than reproduced.

Start here

New to OQCL? You do not need to read everything. Follow the six steps below in order. Your first hour is: read Chapter 1, then run the Quantum Coin experiment.

Download the Student Manual (PDF, 48 pages)

What to do, in order

  1. Read Chapter 1. It gives you the language used everywhere else: amplitudes, probabilities, and phase. Open Chapter 1 →
  2. Run your first experiment, Q01 Quantum Coin. It takes 15–25 minutes on a simulator and needs no hardware. Follow the step-by-step guide →
  3. Choose your project. Open the project ladder and pick the smallest project that extends what you already understand. Each project page names the chapter to read first.
  4. Apply by email before graded or QPU work. Send the project ID, project title, your name, and a short plan to saghiria@wpunj.edu, then wait for instructor approval.
  5. Do the experiment the OQCL way. Write a prediction, reproduce the baseline on a simulator, change exactly one variable for your extension, and analyze the result with a number, not only a picture.
  6. Submit. Put your notebook, raw data, figures, and report in one folder and open a Pull Request. GitHub submission guide →

Common questions

Do I need a real quantum computer?

No. Most projects run on a simulator. Hardware is optional unless your instructor explicitly requires it, and any QPU run needs approval first.

Which software should I use?

Any suitable quantum simulator or computing environment. OQCL is platform-neutral. Guide 1 shows one working option so you are not stuck choosing.

Do I have to finish all 12 chapters before a project?

No. Read the prerequisite chapter listed on your project page. Read in order only if the earlier material is new to you.

What if my prediction turns out wrong?

That is acceptable. A failed prediction is fine when the experiment is reproducible and the analysis is sound. Explain what you expected, what happened, and why they differ.

How much time will a project take?

From 15–25 minutes for the first micro-experiment to 5–10 hours for the capstone. The project ladder lists the time for each one.

Who do I ask when I am stuck?

Email your instructor at saghiria@wpunj.edu with the project ID and a short description of the problem.
